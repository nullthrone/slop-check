<section class="home-section" aria-labelledby="what">
<div class="home-section__head">
<p class="eyebrow">What it does</p>
<h2 id="what">Style patterns, described with evidence</h2>
</div>
<div class="home-section__body prose" markdown="1">

slop-check reads a text and reports patterns that occur far more often in language-model output
than in human writing. Generic openers and antithesis templates are typical examples. Each finding names the pattern, quotes the passage verbatim and counts the hits against
the pattern's threshold. The weights of the triggered patterns add up to a score, and the score
maps to a verdict of `GREEN`, `YELLOW` or `RED`.

A finding describes the style of a passage. It makes no claim about who or what wrote it. People
use these patterns too, and edited model output can be free of them.

The catalog covers English and German text. For images, slop-check supplies prevention rules
that turn a request into an art-directed prompt. Image patterns can be audited as observations;
they carry no score weight until an image benchmark exists. Web pages get the same treatment:
the skill checks a screenshot or the page source for the stock look of generated landing pages and
applies design rules when it builds a page; these patterns are not scored yet either.

</div>
</section>

<section class="home-section" aria-labelledby="admission">
<div class="home-section__head">
<p class="eyebrow">Admission</p>
<h2 id="admission">How a pattern earns its weight</h2>
</div>
<div class="statement" markdown="1">

A pattern counts toward the score only after measurement shows that it triggers much more often
in AI text generated by CI than in human texts published before November 2022. Contributors
cannot promote a pattern themselves, and a pattern that stops passing the measurement loses its
weight.

</div>
</section>
