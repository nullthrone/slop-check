# Governance

This page explains how a pattern enters the catalog, how it earns a weight in the score and how
it loses that weight again. The numbers come from [`governance/thresholds.yaml`](../governance/thresholds.yaml);
if this page and that file disagree, the file wins. The technical contract is
[architecture.md](architecture.md), sections 5 and 6.

## What counts as a slop pattern

A slop pattern is a writing or image pattern that appears clearly more often in current
language-model output than in comparable human work. Sources and good examples make a pattern
worth testing. Only a measurement against human text decides whether it is scored.

Findings always describe style. A text full of slop patterns may have been written by a person,
and a model can write without any of them. Neither the reports nor the skill ever claim to know
who wrote a text.

## Lifecycle

Every pattern file declares a status. Two more states come from measurement.

| Status | How a pattern gets there | Effect in reports |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------|
| `observation` | Declared. Needs one source. A detector is optional. | Left out of reports and the score. Kept as a lead for research. |
| `candidate` | Declared. Needs two independent sources, a detector and, per language, at least two positive and two negative examples. Must pass the pull request gate. | Reported as an observation with weight 0. |
| `core` | Derived. A candidate becomes core when the scheduled measurement shows it meets the core criteria. | Counts in the score with a measured weight. |
| `fading` | Derived. A core pattern that clearly stops standing out. | Reported with weight 0, like a candidate. |

Contributors cannot declare `core`. The one exception is the initial core: the patterns listed
under `initial_core` in `governance/thresholds.yaml` start as `core` with a provisional weight
taken from that list. The first measurement that covers such a pattern replaces the provisional
weight with a measured one, or moves the pattern to `fading`. While any weight in a report is
provisional, the report says so, and the verdict thresholds come from `verdict_provisional`
until measured thresholds exist.

## Independent sources

Two sources count as one when they share a publisher or when one is derived from the other
(the `derived_from` field, followed through every step). Practitioner lists often copy each
other, so ten blog posts can still be a single source. Observations made by the maintainers
(`own-observation`) never count toward independence.

## What the gate measures

Measurement uses two corpora that no contributor controls:

- Human documents published before November 2022, drawn from pinned public datasets: web pages
  (including marketing and blogs), encyclopedia articles and answers from Q&A sites. The texts
  are fetched at run time and never committed; branch `data` stores only their hashes.
- Documents generated by CI from fixed prompts on the same topics, by several model families.
  The models are picked each week from rules in `evals/models.yaml`, so new model versions enter
  the measurement without a code change.

Every document is assigned by a hash of its id to one of two halves. The dev half serves pull
requests. The holdout half serves the monthly measurement that promotes and demotes patterns.
A contributor who tunes a detector against the dev half gains nothing on the holdout half.

For each pattern and language the measurement reports:

- `fpr_human`, the share of human documents in which the full rule fires (detector, threshold and
  any dependency on other findings),
- `hit_ai`, the same share in model output (also broken down by model family),
- `lift`, the ratio of the two, smoothed so that a pattern with no human hits does not get an
  infinite value.

## Pull request gate

Two checks run on every pull request, and both are required on `main`.

`ci-gate` runs for every pull request. Besides lint, tests, schema validation and the example
checks it measures each changed text pattern with a deterministic detector on the dev half:

- A candidate passes when `fpr_human` stays at or below 0.15, overall and in each language.
- A pattern that scores today must still meet the core criteria on the dev half after the edit.
  That includes the initial core.

`llm-gate` covers patterns whose detector is a rubric for a language model. Two judge models from
different families read each example; a hit counts only when both agree. The positive examples
must be recognized at a rate of at least 0.8 and the negative examples flagged at a rate of at
most 0.2 (each example is judged without itself in the prompt). The dev-half rules above apply
as well.

Until branch `data` holds a corpus, the corpus-based parts of both gates are skipped with a notice.

## Why your own examples are not enough

Examples prove that the detector does what its author intended. They cannot show that the
pattern is rare in human writing, because the author chose them. A regular expression that
matches all five positive examples may also fire on a third of all human web pages. The human
corpus catches that before the pattern can affect anyone's verdict.

## Core criteria

The monthly measurement on the holdout half promotes a pattern to core when all of these hold:

- `fpr_human` at most 0.05, overall and in every language,
- `lift` at least 2.0,
- lift at least 1.5 in at least two model families,
- at least 10 model documents in which the pattern fires,
- at least 100 human documents in every measured language.

The weight is the natural logarithm of the lift, clamped to the range 0.5 to 3.0. The verdict
thresholds are measured too: YELLOW starts at the 90th percentile of human document scores,
RED at the median score of model documents.

## Fading

Models change. A pattern that was typical of one model generation can disappear from the next
(the em dash is a common example). A core pattern moves to `fading` when its lift drops to 1.5 or
below, or when it fires in 8 % or more of human documents. The gap between these values and the
core criteria keeps a pattern from switching back and forth between two runs. A fading pattern
stays in the catalog and in reports with weight 0, and it can return to core when a later
measurement supports it.

An edited pattern loses its measurement until the next run measures the new version, because a
measurement belongs to the exact content it was taken from (it stores a hash of the pattern).

## Budget and transparency

Language-model calls go through OpenRouter with one key and a hard cap of 25 EUR per month,
enforced in code before every call. Each pull request may spend at most 0.50 USD, a scheduled
measurement at most 12 USD and a conformance run at most 6 USD. When the money or the key is
missing, candidates still pass the LLM gate with a notice (they carry no weight anyway), and edits
to scored LLM-judged patterns fail until the check can run.

Everything the bots produce is public on branch `data`: the spend ledger, the model lockfile,
each measurement, a readable report per run and the conformance results. Every measurement names
the exact model versions it used. A monthly issue titled "Measurement report YYYY-MM" summarizes
each run.

## Appeals

If you think a measurement treats a pattern unfairly, or a threshold is set badly, open an issue.
Name the pattern id, link the measurement report and explain your argument. Changes to thresholds, judges or
corpora go through a pull request that the code owner reviews, because they change the gate for
everyone.
